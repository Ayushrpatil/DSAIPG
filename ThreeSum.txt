Why the Quadratic Method Works
The quadratic method for solving the ThreeSum problem is an optimized approach that improves efficiency compared to the cubic approach by leveraging sorting and the two-pointer technique.
Key Reasons Why the Quadratic Method Works
1. Sorting for Efficiency

   * The array is first sorted (if not already), allowing a systematic search for valid triplets.
   * Sorting takes O(Nlog⁡N)O(N \log N), but it is a one-time cost.
   2. Fixing a Middle Element

      * Instead of using three nested loops (O(N3)O(N^3)), we fix one element at index jj and use two pointers (left and right) to find the other two elements.
      3. Two-Pointer Technique (O(N)O(N) per fixed element)

         * The two pointers (left at the start, right at the end) move towards each other while searching for pairs that sum to −a[j]-a[j].
         * If the sum is too small, move left rightward.
         * If the sum is too large, move right leftward.
         * If the sum is zero, record the triplet and move both pointers (while avoiding duplicates).
         4. Overall Complexity: O(N2)O(N^2)

            * Since we iterate through NN fixed elements and perform an O(N)O(N) two-pointer search for each, the final complexity is O(N2)O(N^2)—a significant improvement over the cubic O(N3)O(N^3) approach.
Why It’s Better
            * The cubic method blindly checks all possible triplets, leading to redundant checks.
            * The quadratic method systematically narrows down the search space using sorted order and the two-pointer method.
            * It efficiently finds unique triplets while avoiding unnecessary operations, making it practical for larger datasets.
This approach strikes a balance between correctness and efficiency, making it a widely used solution for ThreeSum problems.
